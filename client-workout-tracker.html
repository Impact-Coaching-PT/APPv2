General training guidance. This is educational — everyone's body, injury history, and goals are different. For a plan built specifically around you, see the personal training offer below.
Training Splits▾

Full Body

Train your whole body each session, 2-3x a week. Best for beginners and anyone short on days — every muscle gets hit often, and there's less to plan.

Upper / Lower

Split into upper-body days and lower-body days, usually 4x a week. A good middle ground — more volume per muscle than full body, still simple to follow.

Push / Pull / Legs (PPL)

Push day (chest, shoulders, triceps), Pull day (back, biceps), Legs day. Can run 3x or 6x a week (repeating the cycle twice). Popular because the grouping makes sense — the muscles used work together in real lifts.

Bro Split

One muscle group per day (e.g. chest Monday, back Tuesday...). Allows a lot of volume per muscle but each one is only trained once a week — better suited to more advanced lifters with 5-6 days available.

Which Muscles Train Together▾

Push

Chest, Shoulders, Triceps — all doing the "pushing" motion in pressing exercises.

Pull

Back, Biceps — the "pulling" motion in rows and pull-ups.

Legs

Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes, Calves — usually trained together as a single session given how demanding leg work is.

Core

Can be trained almost daily in small amounts — it recovers faster than bigger muscle groups.
Rest Between Sets▾
Heavier, lower-rep work (strength/power) needs longer rest — 3-5 minutes — to let your nervous system recover for the next set. Lighter, higher-rep work (hypertrophy/endurance) needs less — 30-90 seconds — since the goal is more about volume and fatigue than maximal output each set. If you're regularly gasping for the next set and your numbers are dropping off fast, you probably need more rest, not less.
Progressive Overload▾
The core principle behind getting stronger or bigger: your body only adapts if you gradually ask more of it over time — a bit more weight, a bit more reps, or an extra set, week to week. Doing the exact same weights and reps forever eventually stalls progress. Small, steady increases beat big jumps that risk form breaking down.
Warm-Up & Form Basics▾
A few minutes of light cardio plus 1-2 lighter ramp-up sets before your working weight helps prevent injury and primes the movement pattern. Form comes before load — a lighter weight done well builds more long-term progress than a heavier one done badly. If in doubt, film a set and check it, or ask a coach.
